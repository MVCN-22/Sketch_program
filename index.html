Guide

Everything is in millimetres, with the origin at the bottom left. The preview updates as you type; scroll to zoom and drag to pan. The grid shows 1 cm and 5 cm on paper at the drawing scale.

Standard sections

Pick a type and fill in the fields. Layer tables take one row per layer, from the outer face inwards; gap is the clear distance to the layer before it (blank = the minimum spacing). Edit as custom copies the section, reinforcement included, into the custom editor as a starting point.

Custom sections

  • Outline: one vertex per line, x, y. Add an angle for an arc to the next vertex (0, 300, 180 is a half circle), and brk to draw the next edge as a break line. Pasting from Excel works.
  • Openings: rect x y w h, circle cx cy D or poly x1 y1 x2 y2 ….
  • Layer: bars parallel to a face. Bottom and top give horizontal layers, left and right vertical ones. With a blank offset, layers from the same face stack inwards at the minimum spacing.
  • Perimeter: bars around the outline or the openings, with a bar in every corner.
  • Link: follows the outline, the part inside a rectangle or polygon, or runs around an opening. Links are numbered in list order; bars with Inside link no. stay inside that link.
  • Row, circle pattern, single bar, line bar, dimension, text: free placement.

Measuring and editing the drawing

  • Measure: click two points, then move the mouse to place the dimension line and click again. Points snap to corners, edges, bar centres, bar faces and link faces; hold Alt to place a point freely. Choose Along the two points for sloping measurements. Esc or right-click cancels.
  • Add text: click where the text should go and type it.
  • Edit text: click any label, title or dimension to change what it says. For a dimension, an empty text brings back the measured value.
  • Draw: Pencil draws thin freehand lines, Marker colours areas with a broad see-through stroke, Arrow and Circle are dragged out, and Tag places numbered markers (1, 2, 3 …) you can refer to in the text. Pick a colour and a width in the bar under the tools.
  • Move: drag any label, title, text, dimension, drawing or section view to a new place. Dimensions move sideways only, so they keep measuring the same points. Dragging the background pans.
  • Edit: besides the words, the formatting bar sets font, size, bold, italic, underline and colour, as in a word processor. Font on the Drawing tab changes the default for all text.
  • Rebar: Row of bars places bars (cut by the section) along a line you click from start to end, by spacing or number. Bar along a line draws a bar lying in the section plane; click its points and double-click or press Enter to finish. Shift keeps a segment horizontal or vertical. On a custom section the bars go into the reinforcement list, where you can fine-tune them.
  • Changing a bar you drew: with Edit, click the bar. A box opens with its Ø, spacing or number and its coordinates; drag the blue points on the drawing to move the ends or corners, or drag the bar itself to move all of it, and the coordinates follow (in the reinforcement list as well). Remove deletes it. Move never picks up bars, so dragging a label or text next to a bar cannot shift the bar. This works for rows, single bars and line bars from the reinforcement list too.
  • Cut: click two points across the section. A section along the member (A–A, B–B …) appears beside the drawing, showing the concrete with break lines, longitudinal bars as lines, links at their spacing and in-plane bars as dots. Drag the cut line to move the cut, or drag the view to place it.
  • Remove: click a label, title, dimension or drawing to take it off the drawing. Restore brings back the predefined ones; Undo reverses the last change.
  • Scroll to zoom in any mode; drag with the middle mouse button (or hold Space) to pan while measuring. Shortcuts: H move, M measure, T text, E edit, D draw, B rebar, C cut, R remove, Ctrl+Z undo.
  • Measurements belong to the section they were drawn on and appear in every export. They stay where you put them, so check them after changing the geometry.

Saving and sharing

Your work is kept in this browser automatically. Save project downloads a .json file that Open… reads back. Copy link puts the whole section into a link you can send to a colleague.

Checks

Bar labels say whether a group is top or bottom reinforcement, and the lines under the title list the top, bottom and side reinforcement (both can be switched off on the Drawing tab). On a custom section each bar item has a Top or bottom field; auto takes the face of a layer, or otherwise the position: above or below the centroid, with the middle third of the height counted as sides.

Anchorage and lap lengths

Anchorage and laps in the header opens a page of its own for the section you are drawing. Each group of bars (same position, Ø and bond condition) gets lbd and l0 to EN 1992-1-1, 8.4 and 8.7. Set the concrete class, fyk, the steel stress, the share of bars lapped in one section and the partial factors on the left (the defaults are the recommended values; use your National Annex).

  • Click a row to see the calculation written out with the numbers put in, and the section with that group's bars highlighted.
  • For one group you can set every parameter of Table 8.2: the bond condition, tension or compression, the steel stress; α₁ and α₂ from the bar end (straight, bent or hooked, looped) and your own a, c₁ and c (Fig. 8.3; blank uses what the drawing measures); α₃ from K (Fig. 8.4), the transverse bars (Ø, spacing, legs, or a link of the section with one click) and beam or slab; α₄ for a welded transverse bar; α₅ from the transverse pressure p; and α₆ from ρ₁. α₂·α₃·α₅ is kept at 0.7 or more, and α₃ for laps uses ΣAst,min = As·σsd/fyd. Changed rows are marked ✎; Use the page values for this group undoes it.
  • Lap layout: the member seen from the side, one lane per group of bars cut by the section. Bars of the stock length are laid from the left end and lapped by l₀; with ρ₁ below 100 % they are split into sets with laps staggered by 1.3·l₀ (0.3·l₀ clear, Fig. 8.7). The member length, bar length and ends are set on the left, the bar length also per group. Bars lying in the section plane get a lap position along the bar, and the lap is drawn on the group's section picture.
  • Bond condition comes from where each bar sits, as the section is drawn (cast from the top): good for h ≤ 250 mm, within 250 mm of the bottom, or more than 300 mm below the top when h > 600 mm; poor otherwise. Bars running vertically (columns, piles, plan sections of walls) and in-plane bars at 45° or more are good. Edges drawn as break lines count as continuing concrete.
  • α₂ uses cd = min(a/2, c) measured on the drawing (clear distance to the nearest bar, cover to the nearest real face); the smallest cd of a group governs. α₃, α₄ and α₅ are 1, which is on the safe side.
  • Print / PDF prints every group with its workings; Copy table copies the table for Excel. A summary table can also go on the section drawing (ticked on the page).
  • The results are indicative: the steel stress is the value you give, not taken from an analysis.

The status bar shows As, Ac and ρ, with As for each of top, bottom and sides (cm² for counted bars, cm²/m for bars given by spacing), and warns about overlapping bars and clear spacing below max(Ø, dg + 5 mm, 20 mm). It is a sketching aid, not a design check.
