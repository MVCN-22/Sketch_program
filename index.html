Section

Guide

Everything is in millimetres, with the origin at the bottom left. The preview updates as you type; scroll to zoom and drag to pan. The grid shows 1 cm and 5 cm on paper at the drawing scale.

Working with the app

  • Undo and redo (top bar, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y) cover every change: values in the forms, the bar list, the drawing and the anchorage page. Typing in one box counts as one step.
  • Forms are grouped; click a group heading to fold it, and the app remembers which groups you keep open. Greyed-out text in a box is what a blank box means; hover a ? for an explanation. In number boxes ↑ and ↓ step the value (Shift ×10, Alt ×0.1); a value that is not a number turns red.
  • The panel on the left: drag its right edge to make it wider or narrower (double-click for the default), or hide it with « to give the drawing the whole width (» brings it back).
  • The status bar shows the bars and areas on one line; Top / bottom, anchorage opens the rest. Warnings are always shown.

Finding coordinates

  • Rulers along the bottom and left edge of the drawing give the coordinates in mm, the red arrows mark the origin (0, 0), and the box at the bottom left shows x and y under the mouse. Near a corner or a bar it shows that point's coordinates and names it (on a custom section: outline point 3 is the third line of the Outline box). The ruler button next to Fit switches all of this off.
  • # numbers the outline points as they appear in the Outline box, the openings O1, O2 … and the links L1, L2 … (custom sections).
  • Pick a point: the target button next to an x, y pair in a reinforcement item sets both by clicking on the drawing. Draw on the drawing above a list of points replaces the list with points you click. Points snap to corners, bars and edges, and elsewhere to the grid (the step follows the zoom). Shift keeps a line horizontal or vertical, Alt places a point freely, right-click or Backspace takes the last point back, Enter or a double-click finishes, Esc cancels.

Editing shapes on the drawing (custom sections, Edit tool)

  • Outline and openings: with the Geometry tab open, the corners are blue squares. Drag one to move it; drag the + in the middle of an edge to add a corner there; right-click a corner to delete it. Openings can be moved by their centre, rectangles resized by a corner, circles by the diamond on the edge. The Outline and Openings boxes follow, and comments, arcs and break lines on the other lines are kept. Clicking the concrete with Edit opens the Geometry tab.
  • Draw a new outline and Add a rectangular opening on the Geometry tab work by clicking on the drawing.
  • Reinforcement items: select an item in the list (or click its bars) and drag its blue points: the ends of a row, the points of a line bar or link polygon, the corners of a link rectangle, the centre and radius of a circle pattern (the radius point also sets the start angle), a layer (sideways only, which sets its offset), the line of a dimension and a text. The crosshair point moves the whole item. Items that follow the section, such as a link along the outline, have no points to drag.
  • Dragging snaps to corners, bars and the grid; Alt drags freely, Shift keeps it straight, Esc puts it back. Every change can be undone.

Standard sections

Pick a type and fill in the fields. Layer tables take one row per layer, from the outer face inwards; gap is the clear distance to the layer before it (blank = the minimum spacing). Edit as custom copies the section, reinforcement included, into the custom editor as a starting point.

Custom sections

  • One custom section is kept while you work on standard sections. Coming back from a different standard section, a note offers to keep it, start blank or copy that standard section instead. New section or shape… on the Geometry tab starts a new section from a template, copies any standard section, or changes only the shape and keeps the bars. Undo brings the previous section back.
  • Outline: one vertex per line, x, y. Add an angle for an arc to the next vertex (0, 300, 180 is a half circle), and brk to draw the next edge as a break line. Pasting from Excel works.
  • Openings: rect x y w h, circle cx cy D or poly x1 y1 x2 y2 ….
  • Quick reinforcement (top of the Reinforcement tab) fills the list for the outline in one go, at the cover and with the minimum spacing between layers: beam or column (link, bottom, top and side bars), walls, slabs and frames (transverse and longitudinal bars on every straight face, for any shape such as U-frames, box girders or stairs; short end faces can be left out), bars around the outline (columns, piles, round sections) or hollow or box section (links and bars on the outer faces and around the openings). The items it makes are ordinary items you can change, and Undo takes them back.
  • Face layer: bars along chosen faces in any direction, at cover or inside a link: the inner face of a wall, an inclined slab, a web. Give the faces by the number of their first point (# on the drawing shows them; 2-4 for several, all for every straight face, O1 for the faces of an opening), or use Pick a face and Add a face on the drawing. Layers on the same face stack inwards at the minimum spacing, and the ends stop at the cover of the next face, so corners meet cleanly.
  • Layer: bars parallel to a face. Bottom and top give horizontal layers, left and right vertical ones. With a blank offset, layers from the same face stack inwards at the minimum spacing.
  • Perimeter: bars around the outline or the openings, with a bar in every corner and the bars between spread evenly face by face. Under More control: only around one opening, a different Ø for the corner bars, 2 or 3 rings inwards at the minimum spacing (or a gap you give), an extra distance inwards, and faces whose bars are left out (by the numbers # shows). The quickest way to add one is the Rebar tool's Bars around: click inside the section, or inside an opening; it goes inside the link around it if there is one.
  • Bar set: bars one by one, each line x, y with an optional own Ø. With Edit, drag a bar, right-click one to delete it, drag a + to add one between two bars, or click a bar to change its position or Ø in the panel (Previous and Next step through them). Add bars on the drawing adds bars where you click. Make each bar editable on a perimeter, layer, face layer, row or circle pattern turns its bars into a bar set, keeping its label; the bars then no longer follow changes to the outline.
  • Link: follows the outline, the part inside a rectangle or polygon, or runs around an opening. Links are numbered in list order; bars with Inside link no. stay inside that link.
  • List and drawing: the item selected in the list is shown in blue on the drawing, and hovering an item lights up its bars. With Edit, clicking a bar on the drawing opens its item; with Remove, it deletes the item.
  • Row, circle pattern, single bar, line bar, dimension, text: free placement.

Measuring and editing the drawing

  • Measure: click two points, then move the mouse to place the dimension line and click again. Points snap to corners, edges, bar centres, bar faces and link faces; hold Alt to place a point freely. Choose Along the two points for sloping measurements. Esc or right-click cancels.
  • Add text: click where the text should go and type it.
  • Edit text: click any label, title or dimension to change what it says. For a dimension, an empty text brings back the measured value.
  • Draw: Pencil draws thin freehand lines, Marker colours areas with a broad see-through stroke, Arrow and Circle are dragged out, and Tag places numbered markers (1, 2, 3 …) you can refer to in the text. Pick a colour and a width in the bar under the tools.
  • Move: drag any label, title, text, dimension, drawing or section view to a new place. Dimensions move sideways only, so they keep measuring the same points. Dragging the background pans.
  • Edit: besides the words, the formatting bar sets font, size, bold, italic, underline and colour, as in a word processor. Font on the Drawing tab changes the default for all text.
  • Rebar: Row of bars places bars (cut by the section) along a line you click from start to end, by spacing or number. Bar along a line draws a bar lying in the section plane; click its points and double-click or press Enter to finish. Points also snap to the line where a bar of the chosen Ø sits at cover (inside the links), and to its corners, so drawn bars keep the cover. Shift keeps a segment horizontal or vertical. On a custom section the bars go into the reinforcement list, where you can fine-tune them.
  • Changing a bar you drew: with Edit, click the bar. A box opens with its Ø, spacing or number and its coordinates; drag the blue points on the drawing to move the ends or corners, or drag the bar itself to move all of it, and the coordinates follow (in the reinforcement list as well). Remove deletes it. Move never picks up bars, so dragging a label or text next to a bar cannot shift the bar. This works for rows, single bars and line bars from the reinforcement list too.
  • Cut: click two points across the section. A section along the member (A–A, B–B …) appears beside the drawing, showing the concrete with break lines, longitudinal bars as lines, links at their spacing and in-plane bars as dots. Drag the cut line to move the cut, or drag the view to place it.
  • Remove: click a label, title, dimension or drawing to take it off the drawing. Restore brings back the predefined ones; Undo reverses the last change.
  • Scroll to zoom in any mode; drag with the middle mouse button (or hold Space) to pan while measuring. Shortcuts: H move, M measure, T text, E edit, D draw, B rebar, C cut, R remove, F fit the drawing, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo.
  • Measurements belong to the section they were drawn on and appear in every export. They stay where you put them, so check them after changing the geometry.

SOFiSTiK models

SOFiSTiK in the header opens a page of its own for cutting sections out of a 3D model.

  • .dat files: the Teddy input with the structural points, lines and areas (SPT, SLN with SLNB, SAR with SARB) and SYST for the direction of gravity. Open the main file together with the files it includes (pick several at once, or add them afterwards); #include Geometry.dat is then read from the file you opened, and the page lists any included file that is missing. Comments, ;, LET#/STO# variables (also from TEMPLATE blocks), LOOP and IF, #define with $(…) and #define blocks, and units such as 300[mm] are understood. Records of other programs (AQUA, SOFILOAD, ASE …) are skipped, and so are items the page does not need. A model built in SOFiPLUS can be written to a .dat with Export to DAT or EXPO.
  • SAF files (.xlsx): SOFiSTiK's SAF export writes the model as it is in the database, so it works whatever way the input was written. The 2D members (StructuralSurfaceMember with their nodes, thickness and system plane, and their openings) are read; their layers become the groups. This is the safest route for models with geometry the .dat reader cannot follow, for example points given relative to an axis.
  • Beams (lines with a cross-section) are not part of the cuts.
  • Units: coordinates are taken in m and the thickness T is guessed from its values (m or mm), unless the file gives a unit. Check the size shown under the file name, and change them under Units and direction if needed. Up is taken from GDIR.
  • The views: Plan shows the areas from above, coloured by group; 3D can be turned by dragging. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan. Click an area to see its number, group, thickness and side, to leave it out of the cuts, or to put its thickness on the other side of its surface (QREF ABOV or BELO). Groups can be left out of all cuts.
  • Cuts: Draw a cut on the plan and click two points across the structure (they snap to the corners of the areas; Shift keeps the line square to x or y). The cut is vertical and only reaches between the two points; the arrows show the direction it is seen in, and Reverse turns it round. Drag its ends or the line to move it, or type the coordinates. Add a level cut makes a horizontal cut, for walls and columns.
  • The section: every area the cut passes through gives a strip of its thickness, with ends that follow the area's edges; curved and warped areas are cut as surfaces. Areas modelled at their mid-surfaces leave notches where they meet; Close the corners where areas meet fills them. The strips are joined into one outline, and enclosed spaces, such as the cell of a box girder, become openings. If the cut has separate parts, choose the one to use (or click it in the picture).
  • Bars: Draw its bars in the editor opens the section in the custom editor with its 0, 0 at its lowest left corner, and sets a drawing scale to suit its size. Each section keeps its own bars, texts and anchorage settings: opening another section stores the one you were working on. Moving a cut updates its section, bars included, until you change the outline in the editor yourself.
  • The model is kept in this browser and in Save project files. A Copy link carries the sections but not the model.

Saving and sharing

Your work is kept in this browser automatically. Save project downloads a .json file that Open… reads back. Copy link puts the whole section into a link you can send to a colleague.

Checks

Bar labels say whether a group is top or bottom reinforcement, and the lines under the title list the top, bottom and side reinforcement (both can be switched off on the Drawing tab). On a custom section each bar item has a Top or bottom field; auto takes the face of a layer, or otherwise the position: above or below the centroid, with the middle third of the height counted as sides.

Anchorage and lap lengths

Anchorage and laps in the header opens a page of its own for the section you are drawing. Each group of bars (same position, Ø and bond condition) gets lbd and l0 to EN 1992-1-1, 8.4 and 8.7. Set the concrete class, fyk, the steel stress, the share of bars lapped in one section and the partial factors on the left (the defaults are the recommended values; use your National Annex).

  • The table shows every bar group with lbd and l0, its bond as a coloured tag, and both lengths as bars to the same scale (light: anchorage, red: lap), so the long ones stand out. Show the factors adds cd and the products of the α factors. Click a row to work on that group.
  • Lap: the lap seen from the side, to scale along the bars: l0 in mm and in Ø, the bar ends (straight, bent or looped), and, where 8.7.4.1 asks for them (Ø ≥ 20 mm), the outer thirds where the transverse bars go, checked against the section's links. With less than 100 % lapped in one section the next set of bars is drawn with its lap staggered by 1.3 l0, the 0.3 l0 gap and the 0.65 l0 zone. In compression the bars within 4Ø outside the lap ends are marked. The buttons under the picture change the share lapped, the bond, tension or compression and the bar end in one click. How l0 is made shows each step from lb,rqd to l0 as a bar, so you see which factor matters; Rules for this lap lists the spacing, staggering and transverse-bar rules with this group's numbers.
  • Anchorage: the bar anchored past the point where it must carry its full force, with lbd, lb,rqd and lb,min on a ruler, how lbd is made, and the mandrel diameter for bends.
  • Calculation: every factor written out with the numbers put in, the section with the group's bars highlighted, and all the inputs for one group.
  • All diameters: lbd and l0 for Ø6 to Ø40 in good and poor bond with the page settings, ready to copy into a drawing note.
  • For one group you can set every parameter of Table 8.2: the bond condition, tension or compression, the steel stress; α₁ and α₂ from the bar end (straight, bent or hooked, looped) and your own a, c₁ and c (Fig. 8.3; blank uses what the drawing measures); α₃ from K (Fig. 8.4), the transverse bars (Ø, spacing, legs, or a link of the section with one click) and beam or slab; α₄ for a welded transverse bar; α₅ from the transverse pressure p; and α₆ from ρ₁. α₂·α₃·α₅ is kept at 0.7 or more, and α₃ for laps uses ΣAst,min = As·σsd/fyd. Changed rows are marked ✎; Use the page values for this group undoes it.
  • Along the member: the member seen from the side, one lane per group of bars cut by the section (hover a bar or a lap to see where it runs). The member length, bar length and ends can be changed right there, and a bar schedule lists the cut lengths, numbers and weights. Bars of the stock length are laid from the left end and lapped by l₀; with ρ₁ below 100 % they are split into sets with laps staggered by 1.3·l₀ (0.3·l₀ clear, Fig. 8.7). The member length, bar length and ends are set on the left, the bar length also per group. Bars lying in the section plane get a lap position along the bar, and the lap is drawn on the group's section picture.
  • Bond condition comes from where each bar sits, as the section is drawn (cast from the top): good for h ≤ 250 mm, within 250 mm of the bottom, or more than 300 mm below the top when h > 600 mm; poor otherwise. Bars running vertically (columns, piles, plan sections of walls) and in-plane bars at 45° or more are good. Edges drawn as break lines count as continuing concrete.
  • α₂ uses cd = min(a/2, c) measured on the drawing (clear distance to the nearest bar, cover to the nearest real face); the smallest cd of a group governs. α₃, α₄ and α₅ are 1, which is on the safe side.
  • Print / PDF prints every group with its workings; Copy table copies the table for Excel. A summary table can also go on the section drawing (ticked on the page).
  • The results are indicative: the steel stress is the value you give, not taken from an analysis.

The status bar shows As, Ac and ρ, with As for each of top, bottom and sides (cm² for counted bars, cm²/m for bars given by spacing), and warns about overlapping bars and clear spacing below max(Ø, dg + 5 mm, 20 mm). It is a sketching aid, not a design check.
