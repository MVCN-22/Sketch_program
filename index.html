New project

Starts afresh with the defaults. The cross-sections, the SOFiSTiK reinforcement, zones, joins and settings, the drawing marks and the pictures under the plans are cleared. Use Save project first to keep what you have; Ctrl+Z also brings it back until the page is closed.

Every version of the app opened in this browser shares the same storage, so a new version starts with what an older one left behind. Starting afresh shows what this version does by itself.

Guide

Everything is in millimetres, with the origin at the bottom left. The preview updates as you type; scroll to zoom and drag to pan. The grid shows 1 cm and 5 cm on paper at the drawing scale.

Two workflows

The switch at the top left chooses the workflow; the numbered steps next to it are its pages, and each workflow comes back to the step you left it on.

  • Cross-sections: draw any section freely (1 Section), work out its anchorage and laps (2 Anchorage and laps), and see its bars along the member in 3D with a bar schedule (3 3D and schedule).
  • SOFiSTiK model: model and design the structure in SOFiSTiK, then read the model (1 Model); give the required reinforcement from SOFiSTiK's design, per group and element, and sketch zones that need more on a plan of each element (2 Required reinforcement); check the anchorage and laps and set the details (3 Anchorage and details); look at the bars in 3D, fine-tune them and export them to Tekla to finish the small things there (4 3D model and export). This workflow has anchorage settings of its own, so changing the concrete or the bar length here leaves the cross-sections alone, and the other way round.
  • A section cut from the model on step 1 (Draw its bars in the editor) is drawn in the cross-section workflow, and takes its member length from the model.

Working with the app

  • Undo and redo (top bar, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y) cover every change: values in the forms, the bar list, the drawing and the anchorage page. Typing in one box counts as one step.
  • Forms are grouped; click a group heading to fold it, and the app remembers which groups you keep open. Greyed-out text in a box is what a blank box means; hover a ? for an explanation. In number boxes ↑ and ↓ step the value (Shift ×10, Alt ×0.1); a value that is not a number turns red.
  • The panel on the left: drag its right edge to make it wider or narrower (double-click for the default), or hide it with « to give the drawing the whole width (» brings it back).
  • The status bar shows the bars and areas on one line; Top / bottom, anchorage opens the rest. Warnings are always shown.

Finding coordinates

  • Rulers along the bottom and left edge of the drawing give the coordinates in mm, the red arrows mark the origin (0, 0), and the box at the bottom left shows x and y under the mouse. Near a corner or a bar it shows that point's coordinates and names it (on a custom section: outline point 3 is the third line of the Outline box). The ruler button next to Fit switches all of this off.
  • # numbers the outline points as they appear in the Outline box, the openings O1, O2 … and the links L1, L2 … (custom sections).
  • Pick a point: the target button next to an x, y pair in a reinforcement item sets both by clicking on the drawing. Draw on the drawing above a list of points replaces the list with points you click. Points snap to corners, bars and edges, and elsewhere to the grid (the step follows the zoom). Shift keeps a line horizontal or vertical, Alt places a point freely, right-click or Backspace takes the last point back, Enter or a double-click finishes, Esc cancels.

Editing shapes on the drawing (custom sections, Edit tool)

  • Outline and openings: with the Geometry tab open, the corners are blue squares. Drag one to move it; drag the + in the middle of an edge to add a corner there; right-click a corner to delete it. Openings can be moved by their centre, rectangles resized by a corner, circles by the diamond on the edge. The Outline and Openings boxes follow, and comments, arcs and break lines on the other lines are kept. Clicking the concrete with Edit opens the Geometry tab.
  • Draw a new outline and Add a rectangular opening on the Geometry tab work by clicking on the drawing.
  • Reinforcement items: select an item in the list (or click its bars) and drag its blue points: the ends of a row, the points of a line bar or link polygon, the corners of a link rectangle, the centre and radius of a circle pattern (the radius point also sets the start angle), a layer (sideways only, which sets its offset), the line of a dimension and a text. The crosshair point moves the whole item. Items that follow the section, such as a link along the outline, have no points to drag.
  • Dragging snaps to corners, bars and the grid; Alt drags freely, Shift keeps it straight, Esc puts it back. Every change can be undone.

Standard sections

Pick a type and fill in the fields. Layer tables take one row per layer, from the outer face inwards; gap is the clear distance to the layer before it (blank = the minimum spacing). Edit as custom copies the section, reinforcement included, into the custom editor as a starting point.

Custom sections

  • One custom section is kept while you work on standard sections. Coming back from a different standard section, a note offers to keep it, start blank or copy that standard section instead. New section or shape… on the Geometry tab starts a new section from a template, copies any standard section, or changes only the shape and keeps the bars. Undo brings the previous section back.
  • Outline: one vertex per line, x, y. Add an angle for an arc to the next vertex (0, 300, 180 is a half circle), and brk to draw the next edge as a break line. Pasting from Excel works.
  • Openings: rect x y w h, circle cx cy D or poly x1 y1 x2 y2 ….
  • Quick reinforcement (top of the Reinforcement tab) fills the list for the outline in one go, at the cover and with the minimum spacing between layers: beam or column (link, bottom, top and side bars), walls, slabs and frames (transverse and longitudinal bars on every straight face, for any shape such as U-frames, box girders or stairs; short end faces can be left out), bars around the outline (columns, piles, round sections) or hollow or box section (links and bars on the outer faces and around the openings). The items it makes are ordinary items you can change, and Undo takes them back.
  • Face layer: bars along chosen faces in any direction, at cover or inside a link: the inner face of a wall, an inclined slab, a web. Give the faces by the number of their first point (# on the drawing shows them; 2-4 for several, all for every straight face, O1 for the faces of an opening), or use Pick a face and Add a face on the drawing. Layers on the same face stack inwards at the minimum spacing, and the ends stop at the cover of the next face, so corners meet cleanly.
  • Layer: bars parallel to a face. Bottom and top give horizontal layers, left and right vertical ones. With a blank offset, layers from the same face stack inwards at the minimum spacing.
  • Perimeter: bars around the outline or the openings, with a bar in every corner and the bars between spread evenly face by face. Under More control: only around one opening, a different Ø for the corner bars, 2 or 3 rings inwards at the minimum spacing (or a gap you give), an extra distance inwards, and faces whose bars are left out (by the numbers # shows). The quickest way to add one is the Rebar tool's Bars around: click inside the section, or inside an opening; it goes inside the link around it if there is one.
  • Bar set: bars one by one, each line x, y with an optional own Ø. With Edit, drag a bar, right-click one to delete it, drag a + to add one between two bars, or click a bar to change its position or Ø in the panel (Previous and Next step through them). Add bars on the drawing adds bars where you click. Make each bar editable on a perimeter, layer, face layer, row or circle pattern turns its bars into a bar set, keeping its label; the bars then no longer follow changes to the outline.
  • Link: follows the outline, the part inside a rectangle or polygon, or runs around an opening. Links are numbered in list order; bars with Inside link no. stay inside that link.
  • List and drawing: the item selected in the list is shown in blue on the drawing, and hovering an item lights up its bars. With Edit, clicking a bar on the drawing opens its item; with Remove, it deletes the item.
  • Row, circle pattern, single bar, line bar, dimension, text: free placement.

Measuring and editing the drawing

  • Measure: click two points, then move the mouse to place the dimension line and click again. Points snap to corners, edges, bar centres, bar faces and link faces; hold Alt to place a point freely. Choose Along the two points for sloping measurements. Esc or right-click cancels.
  • Add text: click where the text should go and type it.
  • Edit text: click any label, title or dimension to change what it says. For a dimension, an empty text brings back the measured value.
  • Draw: Pencil draws thin freehand lines, Marker colours areas with a broad see-through stroke, Arrow and Circle are dragged out, and Tag places numbered markers (1, 2, 3 …) you can refer to in the text. Pick a colour and a width in the bar under the tools.
  • Move: drag any label, title, text, dimension, drawing or section view to a new place. Dimensions move sideways only, so they keep measuring the same points. Dragging the background pans.
  • Edit: besides the words, the formatting bar sets font, size, bold, italic, underline and colour, as in a word processor. Font on the Drawing tab changes the default for all text.
  • Rebar: Row of bars places bars (cut by the section) along a line you click from start to end, by spacing or number. Bar along a line draws a bar lying in the section plane; click its points and double-click or press Enter to finish. Points also snap to the line where a bar of the chosen Ø sits at cover (inside the links), and to its corners, so drawn bars keep the cover. Shift keeps a segment horizontal or vertical. On a custom section the bars go into the reinforcement list, where you can fine-tune them.
  • Changing a bar you drew: with Edit, click the bar. A box opens with its Ø, spacing or number and its coordinates; drag the blue points on the drawing to move the ends or corners, or drag the bar itself to move all of it, and the coordinates follow (in the reinforcement list as well). Remove deletes it. Move never picks up bars, so dragging a label or text next to a bar cannot shift the bar. This works for rows, single bars and line bars from the reinforcement list too.
  • Cut: click two points across the section. A section along the member (A–A, B–B …) appears beside the drawing, showing the concrete with break lines, longitudinal bars as lines, links at their spacing and in-plane bars as dots. Drag the cut line to move the cut, or drag the view to place it.
  • Remove: click a label, title, dimension or drawing to take it off the drawing. Restore brings back the predefined ones; Undo reverses the last change.
  • Scroll to zoom in any mode; drag with the middle mouse button (or hold Space) to pan while measuring. Shortcuts: H move, M measure, T text, E edit, D draw, B rebar, C cut, R remove, F fit the drawing, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo.
  • Measurements belong to the section they were drawn on and appear in every export. They stay where you put them, so check them after changing the geometry.

SOFiSTiK models

Step 1 of the SOFiSTiK model workflow reads the model. From there you reinforce its elements (step 2), or cut sections out of it for the cross-section workflow.

  • .dat files: the Teddy input with the structural points, lines and areas (SPT, SLN with SLNB, SAR with SARB) and SYST for the direction of gravity. Open the main file together with the files it includes (pick several at once, or add them afterwards); #include Geometry.dat is then read from the file you opened, and the page lists any included file that is missing. Comments, ;, LET#/STO# variables (also from TEMPLATE blocks), LOOP and IF, #define with $(…) and #define blocks, and units such as 300[mm] are understood. Records of other programs (AQUA, SOFILOAD, ASE …) are skipped, and so are items the page does not need. A model built in SOFiPLUS can be written to a .dat with Export to DAT or EXPO.
  • SAF files (.xlsx): SOFiSTiK's SAF export writes the model as it is in the database, so it works whatever way the input was written. The 2D members (StructuralSurfaceMember with their nodes, thickness and system plane, and their openings) are read; their layers become the groups. This is the safest route for models with geometry the .dat reader cannot follow, for example points given relative to an axis.
  • Beams (lines with a cross-section) are not part of the cuts.
  • Units: coordinates are taken in m and the thickness T is guessed from its values (m or mm), unless the file gives a unit. Check the size shown under the file name, and change them under Units and direction if needed. Up is taken from GDIR.
  • The views: Plan shows the areas from above, coloured by group; 3D can be turned by dragging. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan. Click an area to see its number, group, thickness and side, to leave it out of the cuts, or to put its thickness on the other side of its surface (QREF ABOV or BELO). Groups can be left out of all cuts.
  • Cuts: Draw a cut on the plan and click two points across the structure (they snap to the corners of the areas; Shift keeps the line square to x or y). The cut is vertical and only reaches between the two points; the arrows show the direction it is seen in, and Reverse turns it round. Drag its ends or the line to move it, or type the coordinates. Add a level cut makes a horizontal cut, for walls and columns.
  • The section: every area the cut passes through gives a strip of its thickness, with ends that follow the area's edges; curved and warped areas are cut as surfaces. Areas modelled at their mid-surfaces leave notches where they meet; Close the corners where areas meet fills them. The strips are joined into one outline, and enclosed spaces, such as the cell of a box girder, become openings. If the cut has separate parts, choose the one to use (or click it in the picture).
  • Bars: Draw its bars in the editor opens the section in the custom editor with its 0, 0 at its lowest left corner, and sets a drawing scale to suit its size. Each section keeps its own bars, texts and anchorage settings: opening another section stores the one you were working on. Moving a cut updates its section, bars included, until you change the outline in the editor yourself.
  • The model is kept in this browser and in Save project files. A Copy link carries the sections but not the model.

3D and schedule (cross-sections)

Step 3 of the cross-section workflow builds the reinforcement of the section along the member, from what the section and the anchorage page describe.

  • Bars cut by the section run along the member in bars of the stock length, lapped by l0 and, with less than 100 % lapped in one section, staggered in sets by 1.3·l0, exactly as on Along the member. The two bars of a lap lie side by side, the second one towards the inside. At anchored member ends they stop at the cover and get the bar end chosen on the anchorage page: straight, a 90° bend or a loop, around a mandrel of 4Ø (Ø ≤ 16) or 7Ø (Table 8.1N), turned inwards.
  • Links and bars in the section plane are repeated at their spacing, links from s/2 and in-plane bars from s, as in a Cut view. A link without a spacing is drawn at 200 mm; an in-plane bar without one is drawn once, at mid-length. In-plane bars with a lap position (Calculation tab) are split there and lapped by l0.
  • Lengths from the model: when the section was cut from a SOFiSTiK model (and the model is open), the member length is the extent of the areas the cut passes through, measured square to the cut (vertical for a level cut), and the cut is shown where it lies in the member. The lap layout, the anchorage page and the schedule all use this length. Choose the length typed below under Member and lap layout to use your own. A level cut also makes the bars run vertically for the bond condition.
  • The bar schedule gives every different bar a mark, with its shape, number, centre-line length (bends included, rounded up to 10 mm) and weight; the same schedule is on the anchorage page. Hover a row to find its bars in the model, or hover a bar to see its mark and length.
  • Viewing: drag to turn, right-drag (or Shift+drag) to pan, scroll to zoom where the pointer is, double-click a point to turn around it; 3D, Section, Side and Top set the view and F fits it. Concrete, links, the bar types, the laps and the anchorage lengths can be switched on and off, and Show from … to … cuts the model to a part of its length. Colours go by position (top, bottom, sides) or by mark.
  • Exports: an OBJ file of what is shown (in mm, y up) for Rhino, Blender, Revit or a viewer, and the schedule for Excel.

Required reinforcement (SOFiSTiK model, step 2)

  • Groups: the reinforcement every element of a group needs, in cm²/m, upper and lower face, principal and cross direction (or the two directions of the global choice). Blank keeps the group's mesh as it is.
  • Element: pick one in the list (‹ › step through them); Values of its own gives it values that differ from its group. Everywhere is what it needs everywhere; the zones below are what it needs in total where more is needed. Under each value the bars chosen for it: from the bar lists of step 3 and within the largest spacing allowed, the least steel; in a zone the element's mesh counts and the bars added between it are shown with +; red where the bar lists cannot cover it.
  • Sketching zones on the plan of the element: Rectangle (R) drag across it, Polygon (P) click the corners (the first one again, a double-click or Enter finishes, Backspace takes one back), or Strip along … a line where it meets another element (1.5 m each side; step 4 changes the width). Points snap to corners and lines, else to 50 mm; Alt places them freely. Select: click a zone, drag it to move it, Delete removes it; drag the background to pan, scroll to zoom, F fits. The dashed lines are where other elements meet it, and the red arrow is its local x-axis.
  • A picture under the plan: Picture… loads a screenshot of SOFiSTiK's reinforcement plot for the element; Line it up: click a point on the picture and then the same point of the element (a corner), twice, and the picture is scaled, turned and moved to fit. Trace the zones over it. Pictures are kept in this browser only, not in the project file.

Reinforcing the SOFiSTiK model

Step 4 of the SOFiSTiK workflow: the model in 3D with the bars of every element, its bar schedule and the export to Tekla. Everything of steps 2 and 3 can be fine-tuned here: click a group in the panel, or an element in the view.

  • Faces and directions (top of the panel): as in SOFiSTiK, each area's faces are its upper face (against its local z-axis) and lower face, and the layers run in the principal direction (the local x-axis, or at the angle you give from it, towards local y) and the cross direction at right angles, and only these two are offered; by default the cross reinforcement is the outer layer and the principal reinforcement lies inside it. The local axes are read from the model: z along gravity, or against NX/NY/NZ (which give the upward side); x along DRX/DRY/DRZ, or else the global X-axis (Y for areas square to X) laid into the area, turned by DROT. Show draws them (x red, y green, z blue), and the panel says, for a picked element, where they point. The other choice names the faces top and bottom (walls +X / −X) and offers the global directions instead. Switching renames the layers to the other system by the way they run, so the bars stay where they are; layers that still carry names of the other system are renamed the same way when a step of the model is opened. Projects made before this choice keep the global one.
  • Elements: every element of the model, each with its own outline, openings and thickness, gets layers of bars on its two faces. Set them per group, or click an element and give it reinforcement of its own: for each face the cover and the layers from the outside in, each with Ø, spacing and direction (global X or Y, horizontal or vertical in the element's plane, or along or across its longest edge). Faces are top and bottom for slabs, and +X / −X or +Y / −Y for walls. Groups start with a default (Ø12/150 both ways in slabs, Ø10/150 horizontal and Ø12/150 vertical in walls), marked default until you change it.
  • Joining areas: areas that are one piece of concrete but were modelled apart (a slab split into several areas, a wall split by storeys) can be joined into one element, and the bars then run through. Join touching areas in one plane does it for every group at once; or pick an element, choose Join with other areas… and click the areas (a joined one clicked again is taken out, Esc ends). Joined areas may differ in thickness: the bars step at 1:6 where they do, and follow a thickness that varies. Areas more than 25° out of plane, and curved areas, are not joined. Areas in one plane that touch but are not joined are lapped by l0 and listed in the notes.
  • Where elements meet at an angle (a slab on a wall, a wall under a slab, two walls at a corner) the bars ending there run to the far face of the other element and turn along it, with a leg so that lbd is reached from its inner face (at least 10Ø past the bend, mandrel 4Ø or 7Ø); per group or element they can instead run straight to the far face, or stop at the cover. The panel lists for each element how much of its edge is free and what it meets.
  • Free edges can get U-bars (Ø and spacing; legs 2h, at least 300 mm, inside the outer layers; 9.3.1.4), and openings trimmer bars along each side on both faces, running lbd past the corners, and diagonal bars across the corners. Curved and warped areas get their bars along the surface, at the cover from both faces; their lengths are scheduled in 100 mm steps.
  • Runs longer than the stock length are lapped by l0, worked out for each layer's Ø, spacing, cover and bond (top bars of slabs thicker than 250 mm are in poor bond) with the settings of step 3, and staggered by ρ1. Levels from … to … shows one storey; the schedule lists every bar of the model by mark.
  • Tekla Structures (Export): Tekla rebars (.json) writes every bar of the model (or only the isolated element) as its corner points with the bending radius, in the model's coordinates in mm (z up; a model with z down is turned 180° about x). The script (.py) reads it into the Tekla model that is open, through the Tekla Open API: run py -m pip install pythonnet once, then py tekla_rebars.py file.tekla.json. Bars of one shape at one spacing become a rebar group (stretch it, change the spacing, or ungroup it in Tekla), all others single rebars; the cover is set so the bar centre lines lie on the exported points; there are no hooks, and laps are two overlapping bars. Each bar needs a concrete part: by default the script attaches to the concrete part of the Tekla model where the bars are, and creates a panel from the area (outline, openings, thickness) where there is none; --concrete find, create or pick choose one way. Grade and size names depend on the Tekla environment: --grade and --size, or CONFIG at the top of the script. The script writes a log of what it created, and --remove with that log deletes it again before a new import.
  • Check a section: Check a section through it (an element) or a click on the section through a zone first asks where: a red plane, marked A–A with arrows the way it is seen, follows the pointer over the element; click where the cut goes (Enter takes the place shown, Esc cancels). It then opens a strip of it there, about four bar spacings wide, as a custom section in the cross-section workflow: the bars across the cut as dots at their spacing and depth (zone bars between the element's where they go between them), the others as lines. It then gets the same checks, labels, dimensions, anchorage page and exports as any section. The area's local axes are drawn beside it (x red, y green, z blue): the axes lying in the cut as arrows, the one along the cut bars as ⊙ (towards you) or ⊗ (away); they are ordinary drawing marks, to move or remove. The section takes the thickness there and the zones that cover that point. It is a copy: changes there do not go back to the model. The editor says where the cut is; Show it in the 3D model goes back and frames it, and the cut stays in the 3D view (red, under Show) and on the plan of step 2. Undo brings back the section you had before.
  • Isolate (the button in the view, I, or under Show): only the picked element, or every element of the picked group, is shown as it is, and the view frames it; the other elements are faded (glass-grey bars, faint concrete) or hidden (only faint concrete). Click another element, also a faded one, to isolate that one instead. I or Esc ends it.
  • Required areas: each layer row has an A_s,req column for the required reinforcement in cm²/m. Leave Ø, s or both blank and they are chosen to cover it, with the least steel and then the wider spacing, from the diameters and spacings listed on step 3 and within the largest spacing of EN 1992-1-1 (slabs: 3h and 400 mm for the first layer of a face, 3.5h and 450 mm for the others; walls: 400 mm horizontal, 3h and 400 mm vertical). With Ø and s both given the row is checked. What was chosen is listed under the form, and on step 3; a requirement that cannot be met is shown in red and in the notes.
  • Zones with more reinforcement: pick an element and, under Zones, draw a zone on the plan of the element (drag a rectangle; it snaps to corners and to 50 mm, Alt places it freely), or add a strip along a line where the element meets another one, such as over a wall under a slab or along a supported edge, with a width each side. A rectangle can be turned into a polygon for L-shaped zones, and coordinates can be typed (x, y for slabs; along the wall and z for walls, in the model's unit). Drag a zone on the plan to move it. Each zone has its own layers for each face, and its bars are either
    • in addition to the element's bars: a layer in the direction of one of the element's layers, at that spacing or a multiple of it, goes between the element's bars (Ø16/150 between Ø12/150 gives 75 mm centres), others go in a layer further in; they run lbd past the zone, or only to its edge; or
    • instead of the element's bars: on each face where the zone has layers, the element's bars stop at the zone edge and the zone's bars run l0 past it to lap with them.
    With a required area in a zone whose bars are in addition, the row is the total needed there: the element's bars of that direction count, and the zone adds only what they lack, between them (in every gap or every other gap); if they are enough, no bars are added. This is the usual base mesh plus top-up over supports or in fields. Zone bars that reach an edge meeting another element are anchored into it like the element's bars. Zones are shaded in their colour on the faces they have bars on, their bars are drawn in that colour, the schedule names them, and a section through the zone shows its bars beside the element's. Click a zone, or one of its bars, to edit it. Zones cannot be placed on curved areas.

Anchorage and details (SOFiSTiK model)

Details per group: the cover of the upper and lower face, how bars end where an element meets another one (anchored with a bend, straight to the far face, or stopped at the cover), U-bars at free edges and the trimmer and diagonal bars at openings. Elements with values of their own keep them (step 4).

Step 3 of the SOFiSTiK workflow, with settings of its own: concrete class, fyk, partial factors, steel stress, share lapped in one section ρ1, bond (from position, or all good or all poor) and the bar length. Copy the settings of the cross-sections takes them over in one go. Projects saved before this version keep the settings they were made with.

  • Lap and anchorage lengths: one row for each Ø, spacing, cover and bond in the model, with cd, lbd, l0, the number of bars and laps, the bar ends bent into other elements, and where they are.
  • Elements and Zones: thickness, reinforcement, zones, edges, laps and weight of each element, and each zone with its area, bars and weight. Click a row to open it on step 2.
  • Required and provided reinforcement: every layer given a required area, with the bars chosen (or checked), what the element's bars give in a zone, the area provided and the percentage of the requirement.
  • Copy tables puts the lengths and the bar schedule on the clipboard for Excel; Print / PDF prints the tables, the schedule and the lengths for all diameters.

Saving and sharing

New… starts afresh with an empty project (optionally keeping the SOFiSTiK model file open); Ctrl+Z brings the old one back. The label next to the name shows which version of the app is running; all versions opened in one browser share its storage, so starting afresh is the way to see what a new version does by itself.

Your work is kept in this browser automatically. Save project downloads a .json file that Open… reads back. Copy link puts the whole section into a link you can send to a colleague.

Checks

Bar labels say whether a group is top or bottom reinforcement, and the lines under the title list the top, bottom and side reinforcement (both can be switched off on the Drawing tab). On a custom section each bar item has a Top or bottom field; auto takes the face of a layer, or otherwise the position: above or below the centroid, with the middle third of the height counted as sides.

Anchorage and lap lengths

Step 2 of the cross-section workflow is a page of its own for the section you are drawing (the SOFiSTiK model has its own, above). Each group of bars (same position, Ø and bond condition) gets lbd and l0 to EN 1992-1-1, 8.4 and 8.7. Set the concrete class, fyk, the steel stress, the share of bars lapped in one section and the partial factors on the left (the defaults are the recommended values; use your National Annex).

  • The table shows every bar group with lbd and l0, its bond as a coloured tag, and both lengths as bars to the same scale (light: anchorage, red: lap), so the long ones stand out. Show the factors adds cd and the products of the α factors. Click a row to work on that group.
  • Lap: the lap seen from the side, to scale along the bars: l0 in mm and in Ø, the bar ends (straight, bent or looped), and, where 8.7.4.1 asks for them (Ø ≥ 20 mm), the outer thirds where the transverse bars go, checked against the section's links. With less than 100 % lapped in one section the next set of bars is drawn with its lap staggered by 1.3 l0, the 0.3 l0 gap and the 0.65 l0 zone. In compression the bars within 4Ø outside the lap ends are marked. The buttons under the picture change the share lapped, the bond, tension or compression and the bar end in one click. How l0 is made shows each step from lb,rqd to l0 as a bar, so you see which factor matters; Rules for this lap lists the spacing, staggering and transverse-bar rules with this group's numbers.
